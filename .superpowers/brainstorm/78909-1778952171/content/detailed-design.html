Case Study Sections

01. Abstract & Metadata

Title: Empowering BLV Museum Visitors: From Information Transfer to Negotiable Interpretation

Summary: Moving beyond fixed audio descriptions to a conversational paradigm that returns interpretive authority to blind and low-vision patrons.

Team: Atharva Nayak, Arnav Sharma, Nisheta Gupta

02. The Critique: The Problem of "Fixed" Meaning

Explain the difference between an audit and a critique. Use the "lived experience" of BLV patrons to show why current museum standards (WCAG) aren't enough for meaningful art engagement.

03. The Analysis: 14 Major Museums

Present the "Interpretive Scores" and the three structural mechanisms: Ordering (interpretive claims before perceptual ones), Certainty (facts vs possibilities), and Omission (leaving out details that allow for self-interpretation).

04. The Solution: Negotiable Interpretation

Introduce the design paradigm. Explain "Positioning" (declaring an interpretive stance) and "Reframing" (pushing back on the system's framing).

05. The Design Probe: Conversational UI

Show the flow of the conversational prototype. Stances: Technical, Emotional, Contextual, Musical. Visualizing how these allow the visitor to "negotiate" meaning.

06. Conclusion & Individual Reflection

Reflect on "Accessibility as a Power Question". Why cultural sustainability depends on redistributing interpretive agency.
